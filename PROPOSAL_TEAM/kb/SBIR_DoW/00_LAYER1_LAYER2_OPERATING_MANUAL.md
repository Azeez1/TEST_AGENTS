## PART 1 — FEYNMAN (plain English)

### Layer 1 (the BAA Preface) is the membership card application

SBIR is **venture capital where the government is the LP and the customer**. They want new tech built without paying full development cost. They write stage-gated checks:

- **Phase I** — small bet to see if the idea works
- **Phase II** — bigger bet to build a real prototype
- **Phase III** — unlimited check to deploy, sole-source, no competition, 20-year DFARS 252.227-7018 IP exclusivity

In exchange, you give them a **20-year license to use the tech for government purposes only**. You keep the patent. They cannot resell your IP. They cannot give it to your competitors. Nothing else in venture finance even comes close.

The BAA Preface is one document that says: *"Before we even read your proposal, you must prove you are a real US small business, your lead scientist actually works for you, you registered in our 4 portals, you submitted through DSIP on time, and you told us the truth about who owns you."* Break any of those and your proposal literally does not exist — not rejected, just not seen.

### Layer 2 (Component Instructions) is the per-buyer hiring policy

8 different jobs at 8 different companies. They all follow federal HR law (the BAA). But each company has its own dress code, interview style, salary band, what they value, and what kills your application.

- **Army** — the drill sergeant. 7 pages, no fluff, prove a soldier touched your prototype.
- **DARPA** — wants moonshots. White paper + slide deck (weird format). No tolerance for commercialization buzzwords.
- **DLA** — the logistics manager. "Highly Acceptable" proposals get invited to give an oral pitch — Go/No-Go round.
- **Navy** — the careful engineer. Strict template you must download from navysbir.com. Use the wrong template, instant rejection.
- **SOCOM** — the operator. 10 pages max, OTA contract (not standard FAR), prototype-first.
- **DAF / Air Force** — the startup-accelerator-trying-to-act-corporate. Heavy AI/autonomy, every topic is ITAR.
- **DHA** — the doctor. Bring your IRB and ACURO approvals or stop talking.
- **SCO** — the spy. Classified work, US-owned, NISPOM compliance.

Each Component Instructions doc says: *"The BAA was the federal floor. We add our own ceiling. Read OUR rules or you wrote a proposal for someone we don't represent."*

## PART 2 — FIRST PRINCIPLES (irreducible truths)

### Layer 1 — 8 binary gates + 7 volumes + 4 hidden truths

**8 binary eligibility gates** (any one fails = proposal does not exist):

1. **Small?** ≤500 employees, US-owned >50% by individuals or other small businesses
2. **American?** All work in US, no foreign country of concern ties (PRC, DPRK, Russia, Iran)
3. **Work-doer?** Prime performs ≥2/3 of Phase I, ≥50% of Phase II (direct + indirect costs)
4. **PI primary?** Principal Investigator's primary employment is the prime (>50% of 40-hr week)
5. **Responsive?** You're answering a real posted topic — no unsolicited proposals
6. **On time?** Submitted via DSIP before close; DSIP latency is your fault
7. **CMMC-compliant?** Your level matches the topic's required level (L1 / L2 / L3)
8. **Disclosed?** Vol 7 Foreign Affiliations webform completed truthfully

**7 mandatory proposal volumes** (every SBIR proposal, every component):

| Vol | Name | What it is | Lives where |
|-----|------|------------|-------------|
| 1 | Cover Sheet | DSIP form, includes tech abstract (≤3000 chars) + commercialization summary | DSIP form |
| 2 | **Technical** | The actual pitch — length per Layer 2 | PDF upload |
| 3 | Cost | DSIP cost form + supporting backup | DSIP form |
| 4 | Commercialization Report (CCR) | Pulled from SBIR.gov by Firm Admin | Firm-level form |
| 5 | Supporting Docs | Letters of support, data rights assertions, joint venture certs, etc. | PDF upload |
| 6 | Fraud/Waste/Abuse Training | 3-page PDF, completed by designated Proposal Owner | DSIP attestation |
| 7 | Foreign Affiliations Disclosures | **Webform only** — PDF uploads rejected | DSIP webform |

**4 hidden truths the BAA doesn't tell you plainly:**

1. **They're buying optionality, not products.** Phase I = right to do Phase II. Phase II = right to do Phase III. The real product is your team's capacity to execute, not the prototype.
2. **They evaluate the team, not the idea.** All three formal criteria (tech merit, qualifications, commercial potential) are proxies for one question: *"Can THESE specific people actually ship this?"*
3. **Phase III is the entire game.** Unlimited $, sole-source (no recompete), 20-year DFARS exclusivity, full IP retention. Phase I+II are qualifier rounds.
4. **DSIP "complete" status is a lie.** Hitting "complete" does NOT mean your component-specific requirements are met. Cross-check the Layer 2 doc separately.

### Layer 2 — 7 variables + 1 universal skeleton

**7 variables that differ per component** (encode every component as a row of these):

| # | Variable | Why it matters |
|---|----------|----------------|
| 1 | **Phase I page limit** | How compressed your story must be (Army 7 → DHA 20 → DAF 35) |
| 2 | **Phase I cost cap** | DLA $100K → Army $300K → DARPA $250K → DHA $300K |
| 3 | **Phase I duration** | 3 months → 12 months depending on topic |
| 4 | **Evaluation rubric** | Some publish it (Army's 4-tier 6-criterion is the cheat sheet). Some hide it. |
| 5 | **TABA participation** | Free $ for commercialization/CMMC/DCAA support. Yes: Army, DARPA, Navy. No: DAF, DLA, DHA, SCO, SOCOM. |
| 6 | **Phase II Enhancement** | Component matches outside capital dollar-for-dollar. Army does up to $500K. |
| 7 | **Special gates** | Slide deck rounds, oral pitches, xTech prizes, white paper format, classified-only |

**Universal 7-section skeleton** every Component Instructions doc follows:

1. Program Description — what they care about specifically
2. Certifications & Registrations — how to register correctly with them
3. **Proposal Preparation** — how they want it formatted (MOST IMPORTANT)
4. Selection & Evaluation — how they score you
5. Additional Considerations — contract type, deliverables, special rules
6. Proposal Submission — their contacts, their deadlines
7. Topic List — what they're buying this release (usually at the back)

Master this skeleton once → you can decode any new component doc in under an hour.

## PART 3 — THE PROPOSAL WRITER'S OPERATING MANUAL

### A. Universal pre-writing gates (do once per company, then forget)

```
[ ] SAM.gov registered — UEI + CAGE active
[ ] SBA Company Registry — SBC Control ID current
[ ] DSIP account — Firm Admin set, Firm-level forms complete, Login.gov linked
[ ] Vol 4 CCR — generated from SBIR.gov dashboard, uploaded to DSIP Firm Forms
[ ] CMMC posture — Level 2 self-assessment posted to SPRS (110 NIST 800-171 Rev 2 controls)
[ ] Foreign affiliations — clean profile, Africa-related plans in separate entity
[ ] PI candidate — primary employment (>50% of 40-hr week) is Dux Machina
[ ] Vol 6 FWA training — designated Proposal Owner completed within last 12 months
```

If any one of these isn't true on submission day, the proposal cannot be submitted. Fix them ONCE, refresh annually.

### C. Volume-by-volume writing rules

**Vol 2 (Technical) — 12 sections, 1 hidden question each:**

| # | Section | Page allocation | What you write | Hidden evaluator question |
|---|---------|-----------------|----------------|---------------------------|
| 1 | Problem/Opportunity | 10% | Topic problem in your own words + why it matters now | "Do they actually understand the problem?" |
| 2 | Technical Objectives | 10% | Enumerated objectives, testable, tied verbatim to topic | "Did they read the topic or shotgun a template?" |
| 3 | Statement of Work | 30–40% | Phase I tasks, methods, schedule, deliverables | "Is the science real or vaporware with buzzwords?" |
| 4 | Related Work | 10% | Prior work, prior performance, what's already proven | "Have they shipped something like this before?" |
| 5 | Phase II/III Transition | 10% | How this becomes Phase II, what evidence enables it | "Will this go anywhere?" |
| 6 | Commercialization | 15–20% | First product, customers, market size, $ needed, competitors | "Will the gov still want this in 3 years?" |
| 7 | Key Personnel | 5–10% | PI bio + key team resumes | "Are THESE humans the ones who can ship?" |
| 8 | Foreign Citizens | 1 table | None / yes-with-disclosure | "Security risk?" |
| 9 | Facilities | 5% | What you've got, environmental compliance | "Can they start work day 1?" |
| 10 | Subs/Consultants | 5% | Who, why, % work, POW math closure | "Is the prime really doing the work?" |
| 11 | Prior/Pending Support | "None" or itemized | Other proposals with overlap | "Are they double-dipping?" |
| 12 | Data Rights Assertions | 1 table | DFARS 252.227-7017 restrictions | "What's your IP boundary?" |

**6 writing rules that win:**

1. **Bold every topic/section reference** — `**SOO 3.1.1**`, `**Section L**`, `**Section M factor 2**`. Evaluators scan; bolding makes traceability obvious.
2. **Mirror the topic's language verbatim** in Objectives and SOW. If the topic says "confidence-scored feedback," you say "confidence-scored feedback" — not "calibrated outputs."
3. **Every claim gets evidence.** Don't say "our team is experienced." Say "our team shipped X for Y in 2024, reducing Z by N%." Evaluators score what they BELIEVE about you, not what you assert.
4. **PESTO per paragraph**: **P**osition / **E**xecution / **S**pecifics (bold the topic ref) / **T**ools / **O**utcomes.
5. **POW math must close.** Phase I prime ≥66.7%, Phase II prime ≥50%.
6. **Phase III is addressed in Phase I.** Commercialization section answers: *"At Phase III, who's the program office that buys this, what's their budget, what's the recurring revenue model."* Without a Phase III hypothesis, you score "Satisfactory" max.

**Vol 3 (Cost) — where amateurs leak credibility:**

- Itemize personnel by name + hours + loaded rate
- Subcontractor costs detailed at prime-level granularity
- Travel must have purpose + # trips + origin/destination + length + # personnel
- TABA (if applicable) lives in Cost Vol with provider name, POC, tasks, hours, blended rate
- No fee/profit on TABA; no G&A loading on TABA
- Equipment purchases need a justification narrative

**Vol 5 (Supporting Docs) — kitchen sink, varies per component:**

- Letters of support (NOT contingent on award)
- Joint venture eligibility certification (if applicable)
- DFARS 252.227-7017 data rights assertions (if asserting restrictions)
- DD Form 2345 ITAR certification (if topic is ITAR/EAR)
- DON Supporting Documents template (Navy only)
- VCOC/HF/PEF certification (if majority-owned by VC/PE/HF and component allows)
- Component-specific feasibility doc (for all D2P2 topics)

**Vol 7 (Foreign Affiliations) — DSIP webform, NOT PDF:**

8 standardized questions about ownership, talent programs, IP transfers, foreign country ties. Truthful disclosure ≠ disqualification. Untruthful disclosure = criminal liability under 18 U.S.C. § 1001 (5 years prison, $250K fine).

### D. Hidden scoring rules (the meta-game)

1. **First impression in 30 seconds.** Evaluators read 20+ proposals per topic. By page 2, they've decided if you're a serious contender. Front-load credibility (named past performance, specific tools, named customers).
2. **The team section is graded against the topic, not in isolation.** Tailor resumes per topic — don't reuse generic bios.
3. **"Innovation" is a scoring trap.** Distinguish *novel* (good) from *contrived* (bad). If you're not actually innovating, emphasize *integration*, *scaling*, or *operational maturity* instead.
4. **Commercialization is where AI proposals die.** Generic "we'll license this to gov" = "Satisfactory." Specific "we have an LOI from Customer X at $Y revenue by month Z" = "Superior." Get LOIs before writing.
5. **Visual evidence multiplies credibility.** Process diagram, architecture sketch, prototype screenshot = each one moves you up half a rubric tier.
6. **The 90-day notification window is also a feedback window.** Even on non-selection, request the debrief. Component-specific feedback is competitive intel for the next submission.

## NET — HOW TO WRITE ANY SBIR PROPOSAL IN 5 STEPS

```
1. Verify universal gates (Section A) — one-time, then maintain
2. Fill per-proposal lookup table (Section B) — 30 min per topic
3. Build Vol 2 from 12-section skeleton (Section C, Vol 2 table) — write to hidden question per section
4. Apply Dux Machina overlays (Part 4) — inject named frameworks for differentiation
```
