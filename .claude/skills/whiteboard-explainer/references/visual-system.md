# Whiteboard visual system

The target is visual explanation, not a generic slideshow with a hand pasted on top.

## Core grammar

- White or warm-paper surface.
- Black or charcoal marker line as the dominant visual channel.
- One accent color per concept cluster; two accents maximum in a frame.
- One new visual idea per beat.
- Continuous object identity: the same symbol evolves instead of being replaced arbitrarily.
- Physical causality: the hand or marker action visibly creates, connects, circles, crosses out, or transforms the idea.
- Exact words and numbers are post-production overlays.

## High-retention beat pattern

1. Pattern interrupt: an incomplete, strange, or counterintuitive image.
2. Knowledge gap: the narration asks or implies why it matters.
3. Progressive reveal: every stroke answers part of the question.
4. Visual turn: a line, arrow, scale change, or cross-out reframes the idea.
5. Payoff: the completed diagram resolves into one memorable picture.
6. Final hold: the key image and exact takeaway remain readable.

## Framing

Use a stable overhead or slightly oblique camera. Keep drawing activity inside safe margins. In vertical video, reserve clean zones near the top for a short headline and near the bottom for captions. Do not cover the hand's active drawing area with overlays.

## What to avoid

- long static handwriting;
- more than one sentence of visible copy;
- dense infographics built inside the video model;
- random zooms and camera movement;
- decorative doodles that do not advance the explanation;
- changing hand, marker, paper, or line style between connected scenes;
- ending immediately after the last stroke without a readable hold.
