# Sterling Legal Services — Frequently Asked Questions

## How much does it cost to hire you?

Nothing up front. We work on contingency — we only get paid if we recover money for you. Our fee comes out of the recovery, not your pocket.

## Do I have a case?

That's the question our attorney will answer during your free consultation. We can't determine that on the intake call — every case is different.

## How long do personal injury cases take?

It varies. Simple cases with clear liability and modest injuries can resolve in 6–12 months. Complex cases with disputed fault or serious injuries may take 18–36 months or longer. Our attorney will give you a realistic timeline once they understand the facts.

## What if the at-fault person doesn't have insurance?

We can still help. There are often other sources of recovery — uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage on your own policy, employer liability, or other responsible parties. Don't assume you have no case just because the other person is uninsured.

## What's the deadline to file a personal injury case in North Carolina?

North Carolina's statute of limitations for most personal injury cases is **three years from the date of injury**. Some types of cases have shorter deadlines (e.g., medical malpractice, claims against government entities). If you're close to any deadline, call us immediately — don't wait.

## Should I talk to the other party's insurance company?

Generally no — not until you've spoken to an attorney. Insurance adjusters are trained to minimize what they pay. Anything you say can be used to reduce your settlement. Let us handle communications with them.

## Will I have to go to court?

Most cases settle before trial. But we prepare every case as if it will go to trial — that's what gives our clients leverage in settlement negotiations. If your case does go to court, you'll be prepared and supported every step of the way.

## How much is my case worth?

The honest answer is: it depends. Compensation is calculated based on medical expenses, lost wages, future medical needs, pain and suffering, and other factors. Our attorney will give you a realistic range after reviewing the facts. Be skeptical of any firm that promises a specific dollar amount before they've seen the evidence.

## Can I switch attorneys if I've already hired someone?

Yes. Hiring decisions aren't permanent. If you're unhappy with your current representation, you have the right to seek a second opinion or change firms. We're happy to discuss your situation confidentially.

## What information should I have ready for the consultation?

If you have any of the following, bring them — but don't worry if you don't have everything yet:

- Police or incident report
- Medical records and bills
- Photos of injuries, vehicles, or the scene
- Names and contact info of witnesses
- Insurance information (yours and the other party's)
- Any correspondence with insurance companies

If you don't have any of this, that's okay — we can help you gather it.
