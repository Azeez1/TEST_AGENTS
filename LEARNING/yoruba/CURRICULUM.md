# Professional Yoruba Curriculum

## Mission

Become professionally competent in Standard Yoruba: able to understand, speak, read, and write Yoruba across everyday, cultural, and chosen professional domains with accurate tone, natural repair strategies, and enough cultural judgment to avoid sounding mechanically translated.

This is not a 12-week "learn phrases" plan. It is a proficiency ladder. You move forward by proving capability, not by finishing chapters.

## First Principles

### 1. A language is sound mapped to meaning

Before grammar or vocabulary matters, the ear has to hear the contrasts the language uses. Yoruba uses tone contrastively, so pitch is part of the word. If the tone changes, meaning can change. Therefore the curriculum starts with listening, tone, vowel quality, nasal vowels, and the letters that English does not train you to hear.

Training consequence:
- Every study week includes audio.
- Every new word is learned with tone marks and sound.
- Speaking practice is recorded and corrected.

### 2. Fluency is automatic retrieval under pressure

Knowing a word in a list is not fluency. Fluency means the right sound, word, and sentence pattern comes out while another person is waiting. Therefore you need repeated retrieval, fast drills, and live conversation.

Training consequence:
- Every week has timed speaking.
- Vocabulary is practiced from English to Yoruba and Yoruba to English.
- You repeat fewer patterns more deeply before adding more.

### 3. Grammar is a sentence engine

Grammar is not a pile of rules. It is the machinery that lets you create unlimited sentences from limited parts. A better Yoruba curriculum teaches the smallest sentence engines first: identity, possession, action, negation, questions, time, location, description, sequence, cause, and opinion.

Training consequence:
- Every grammar lesson ends with original sentences about your real life.
- You do not memorize isolated grammar labels before using the pattern.
- You learn repair phrases early so conversation does not collapse.

### 4. Professional skill requires domains

Nobody is "pro" in a language abstractly. A pro can function in specific settings: family conversation, travel, meetings, interviews, faith/culture, business, media, teaching, translation, or community work. Therefore the curriculum has a general core plus one specialization track.

Training consequence:
- After the foundation, you pick one professional domain.
- You build a domain glossary, scripts, and role-plays.
- Native-speaker feedback becomes mandatory.

### 5. Culture changes what the sentence means

The literal words are not the whole message. Greetings, respect forms, kinship terms, names, humor, indirectness, titles, and proverbs carry social meaning. Therefore culture is trained as a communication skill, not as trivia.

Training consequence:
- Every phase includes cultural usage.
- You learn when a phrase is appropriate, not only what it translates to.
- You test speech with native speakers and adjust.

## Target Standard

Use ACTFL-style capability as the external measuring frame:

- Minimum useful professional target: Advanced Low to Advanced Mid in speaking and listening for a chosen domain.
- Strong professional target: Advanced High to Superior behaviors in your domain, with the ability to handle complications, explain viewpoints, narrate across time, and understand unscripted speech.
- Literacy target: read and write connected paragraphs with correct tone marks, underdots, and natural structure.

The practical definition: you can handle real tasks in Yoruba without switching back to English every time the conversation stops being scripted.

## Weekly Time Budget

### Serious Track: 7-10 hours per week

- 5 days: 60-75 minutes solo practice.
- 1 day: 45-60 minutes tutor or language exchange.
- 1 day: 30-45 minutes review, recording, and reflection.

Expected path:
- 3 months: solid beginner.
- 6 months: useful low-intermediate.
- 12 months: strong everyday conversation if practice is consistent.
- 18-24 months: professional competence in a chosen domain.

### Intensive Track: 14-18 hours per week

- Daily audio and speaking.
- 2-3 live sessions per week.
- Regular media immersion.
- Domain work starts earlier.

Expected path:
- 6 months: serious conversational ability.
- 12 months: possible professional-domain competence with strong feedback.

## Daily Training Loop

Use this loop most study days:

1. Ear: 10 minutes tone, vowel, nasal vowel, or consonant contrast.
2. Mouth: 10 minutes shadowing audio, then recording yourself.
3. Memory: 10 minutes spaced retrieval of words and phrases.
4. Engine: 15 minutes building original sentences from one pattern.
5. Input: 15 minutes listening or reading something just above your level.
6. Output: 10-20 minutes speaking or writing something real.

Rule: no day counts unless you produce Yoruba with your mouth or hand.

## Phase 0: Diagnostic And Setup

Length: 1 week.

Goal:
- Establish your real starting level.
- Choose the first domain: family, travel, culture, work, religion, media, or literacy.
- Set up audio sources, flashcards, notebook, and correction workflow.

Tasks:
- Record a 60-second self-introduction attempt, even if it is mostly English.
- Learn the Yoruba keyboard/input method for tone marks and underdots.
- Create a correction log with three columns: error, corrected form, reason.

Gate:
- You can explain your mission and weekly time budget.
- You have a place to store recordings, vocabulary, and corrections.

## Phase 1: Sound And Orthography

Length: 4-6 weeks.

Core skill:
- Hear, read, and reproduce the sound units Yoruba uses to carry meaning.

Focus:
- Alphabet.
- Oral vowels.
- Nasal vowels.
- Underdotted letters: ẹ, ọ, ṣ.
- gb and p as Yoruba consonant contrasts.
- High, mid, and low tones.
- Syllabic nasals.

Practice:
- FSI tone drills.
- Yorùbá Yé Mi alphabet, consonant, vowel, nasal vowel, and tone audio.
- Minimal daily recording.
- Tone-mark copying by hand or keyboard.

Gate:
- Given a short beginner word list with audio, you can mark the high/low/mid contour with reasonable accuracy.
- A native speaker or tutor can understand your practiced words without repeated correction.
- You can explain in plain English why tone is part of spelling and meaning.

## Phase 2: Survival Conversation

Length: 6-8 weeks.

Core skill:
- Open, maintain, and close simple conversations.

Focus:
- Greetings by time of day and social relationship.
- Names, origin, residence, occupation, and language ability.
- Pronouns and basic identity statements.
- Simple actions and objects.
- Yes/no and information questions.
- Repair phrases: repeat, slow down, I do not understand, how do you say.

Practice:
- Scripted dialogues.
- Substitution drills.
- 2-minute tutor conversations.
- Short voice notes.

Gate:
- You can hold a 3-minute conversation about identity, origin, and daily life.
- You can ask at least 20 useful questions.
- You can recover when you do not understand.

## Phase 3: Sentence Engine

Length: 10-12 weeks.

Core skill:
- Generate original Yoruba sentences instead of repeating memorized phrases.

Focus:
- Subject-action-object patterns.
- Negation.
- Possession.
- Location.
- Time expressions.
- Serial actions.
- Descriptions.
- Preferences and opinions.
- Past, present, future, and habitual meanings.

Practice:
- Sentence transformation drills.
- Daily routine narration.
- Picture description.
- "Say the same idea five ways" drills.
- Weekly writing corrected by a native speaker or tutor.

Gate:
- You can describe a normal day, a past event, and a future plan.
- You can produce connected strings of sentences, not just isolated lines.
- You can correct recurring grammar errors from your own correction log.

## Phase 4: Everyday Domains

Length: 12-16 weeks.

Core skill:
- Function comfortably in common life situations.

Domains:
- Family and relationships.
- Food, cooking, eating, hosting.
- Market, money, prices, negotiation.
- Travel, directions, transportation.
- Home, work, school, neighborhood.
- Health, feelings, needs.
- Events, celebrations, visits.

Practice:
- Role-plays.
- Task simulations.
- Listening to slow native speech.
- Short readings.
- Weekly "real conversation" assignment.

Gate:
- You can handle at least five everyday situations without a script.
- You can ask follow-up questions naturally.
- You can summarize a simple story or dialogue.

## Phase 5: Literacy And Culture

Length: 12-16 weeks.

Core skill:
- Read and write connected Yoruba while understanding social meaning.

Focus:
- Tone-marked reading.
- Short paragraphs.
- Names and naming logic.
- Respect terms and titles.
- Proverbs and when to use them.
- Songs, prayers, public speech, and media snippets based on your mission.
- Formal versus casual expression.

Practice:
- Read short texts aloud.
- Copy model paragraphs.
- Write 150-250 words weekly.
- Maintain a glossary of culturally loaded terms.
- Ask a native speaker: "Would this sound natural? When would someone actually say it?"

Gate:
- You can write a corrected one-page personal or domain-specific text.
- You can explain the social function of common greetings and respect forms.
- You can read a short tone-marked passage aloud with understandable rhythm.

## Phase 6: Professional Domain Track

Length: 16-24+ weeks.

Pick one track first. Add others later.

### Family And Heritage Track

Outputs:
- Speak with elders respectfully.
- Explain your life, work, values, family, and plans.
- Understand family stories, names, praise, greetings, and common advice.

Capstone:
- 20-minute family conversation with limited English support.

### Business And Professional Track

Outputs:
- Introduce your company or work.
- Discuss meetings, schedules, services, prices, problems, and next steps.
- Write short professional messages.

Capstone:
- 10-minute simulated business conversation and one written follow-up message.

### Culture, Religion, Or Media Track

Outputs:
- Understand selected songs, sermons, speeches, films, or interviews.
- Build vocabulary around the cultural domain.
- Explain the meaning without flattening it into literal English.

Capstone:
- Present a 5-minute explanation of one cultural text or media clip.

### Literacy And Translation Track

Outputs:
- Read tone-marked texts accurately.
- Translate short passages with notes on ambiguity.
- Write clear Yoruba paragraphs.

Capstone:
- Translate and discuss a one-page text with a native speaker or tutor.

Gate:
- You can perform three real tasks in your chosen domain with a native speaker.
- You can handle interruptions, clarification, and follow-up questions.
- Your correction log shows fewer repeated sound and grammar errors.

## Phase 7: Pro Maintenance

Length: ongoing.

Core skill:
- Keep Yoruba alive through use.

Weekly minimum:
- 1 live conversation.
- 1 listening session with native media.
- 1 written output.
- 1 correction review.
- 1 vocabulary/domain update.

Quarterly capstone:
- Record a 10-minute monologue.
- Hold a 20-minute conversation.
- Write a one-page piece.
- Review all three with a native speaker or tutor.

## Assessment System

Score each area from 0-4 every week:

- Sound: tones, vowels, nasal vowels, rhythm.
- Listening: words, phrases, gist, details.
- Speaking: speed, repair, confidence, originality.
- Grammar: sentence control, negation, questions, time.
- Vocabulary: active recall, domain coverage, natural phrases.
- Literacy: tone marks, underdots, reading aloud, writing.
- Culture: appropriateness, greetings, respect, social meaning.

0 = no control.
1 = with notes.
2 = slow but independent.
3 = natural in practiced settings.
4 = flexible in new settings.

Do not advance a phase until most relevant scores are 3 or higher.

## First 30 Days

### Week 1

- Set up keyboard/input for Yoruba diacritics.
- Study alphabet, oral vowels, nasal vowels, and tones.
- Record baseline self-introduction.
- Begin FSI tone drills and Yorùbá Yé Mi introduction audio.

### Week 2

- Greetings and names.
- 30 core words with audio.
- 10 daily tone-shadowing recordings.
- First tutor/native speaker correction if possible.

### Week 3

- Identity and origin sentences.
- Basic pronouns.
- 20 question-answer pairs.
- 2-minute scripted conversation.

### Week 4

- Repair phrases.
- Simple daily actions.
- 60-second self-introduction from memory.
- Review correction log and choose next weak point.
