Lesson 1: Tone Is Meaning

One idea only: in Yoruba, pitch is not decoration. Pitch helps identify the word. If you want professional-level Yoruba, your ear has to treat tone as part of the word from day one.

First principle: spoken language is sound carrying meaning. Yoruba uses pitch differences to carry meaning. Therefore, learning a Yoruba word without its tone is like learning only part of the word.

The plain-English version

In English, pitch often shows feeling: surprise, doubt, excitement. In Yoruba, pitch can help decide which word was said. So the first job is not to memorize hundreds of words. The first job is to train your ear to hear the shape of the word.

The formal term is tone. Yoruba has high, mid, and low tones. High is usually marked with an acute accent, low with a grave accent, and mid is often unmarked.

bí
High tone
Example meaning in Yorùbá Yé Mi: to give birth to.
bi
Mid tone
Example meaning in Yorùbá Yé Mi: to ask.
bì
Low tone
Example meaning in Yorùbá Yé Mi: to vomit.

Why this changes the curriculum

If tone carries meaning, then vocabulary cannot be "spelling plus English definition." A real vocabulary item has four parts: spelling, sound, tone, and meaning. Leave one out and the word is unstable.

That is why your daily practice starts with audio. You are not doing pronunciation as polish at the end. You are building the foundation.

Practice

Question: If you learn a new Yoruba word from text only, what is missing?

Five-minute drill

  1. Open the Yorùbá Yé Mi tone audio or an FSI tone drill.
  2. Listen once without speaking.
  3. Listen again and trace the pitch with your hand: up for high, level for mid, down for low.
  4. Repeat the sound after the speaker.
  5. Record yourself once. Do not judge the recording yet; just create the feedback loop.

Teach-back

Explain this out loud in your own words as if teaching a smart 12-year-old: why is tone part of the word in Yoruba?
