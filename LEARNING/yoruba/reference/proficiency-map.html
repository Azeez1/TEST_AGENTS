Yoruba Proficiency Map

A quick reference for moving from beginner to professional Yoruba. Advance by performance: what you can do reliably with a listener, not what you have read once.

1. Sound

  • Hear high, mid, and low tone.
  • Produce oral and nasal vowels.
  • Use ẹ, ọ, ṣ, gb, and p accurately.
  • Record speech and accept correction.

2. Survival

  • Greet by time, age, and respect context.
  • Introduce yourself and ask basic questions.
  • Use repair phrases before switching to English.
  • Hold a 3-minute beginner conversation.

3. Sentence Engine

  • Create original statements, questions, and negatives.
  • Talk about time, place, possession, and sequence.
  • Describe a day, a past event, and a future plan.
  • Correct repeated errors from your log.

4. Everyday Domains

  • Handle family, food, market, travel, home, work, and health contexts.
  • Ask follow-up questions naturally.
  • Summarize simple dialogues and stories.
  • Speak without a script in five common situations.

5. Literacy And Culture

  • Read tone-marked texts aloud.
  • Write connected paragraphs with diacritics.
  • Understand names, titles, greetings, and respect forms.
  • Explain social meaning, not just literal translation.

6. Professional Domain

  • Pick one domain first: family, work, culture, religion, media, or literacy.
  • Build a domain glossary and scripts.
  • Practice role-plays with a native speaker.
  • Perform three real tasks in Yoruba.

Daily Loop

Ear, mouth, memory, sentence engine, input, output. A study day only counts when you produce Yoruba by speaking or writing.

Core sources: Yorùbá Yé Mi, FSI Yoruba Basic Course, ACTFL Proficiency Guidelines.
