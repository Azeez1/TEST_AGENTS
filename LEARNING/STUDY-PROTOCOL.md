# Study & Encoding Protocol — the Sung Method (for the 90-Day Sprint)

**Source:** Synthesized by the Oracle from the Justin Sung corpus in EZ's Obsidian vault (Sung = best-covered creator: 119 articles + ~50 concept pages). **Rolls up to:** `Q3-2026-FOCUS-MAP.md` · plugs into `system-design/REP_PLAN.md` + `agentic-engineering/AE-REP-PLAN.md`.

## The law (anchor everything here)
> Stop trying to *remember*. **Simplify, compare, connect, group** — retention is the side effect of that thinking, not of review.

Your rep ritual (notes closed, name the fundamental limit a concept solves) already IS Sung retrieval + higher-order thinking. Missing pieces this adds: an **encode front-end** (new material sticks pass one) + a **self-spacing back-end** (recall compounds over 90 days).

---

## TRACK A — Encode a NEW concept (once, ~15–25 min, notes OPEN)
1. **Prime** — skim for keywords, group them, write the *limit it solves* + a one-sentence 10-year-old explanation. Limit FIRST, mechanism later.
2. **Higher-order first** — ask: What problem? How's it differ from its confusable neighbor? What connects to it? Where does it rank? (Lower-order recall fills in on the way up — faster.)
3. **One analogy** — build a plain-English bridge to something you own, push it against an edge case till it breaks, refine 2–3×.
4. **Compress** — write it as ~2 words + an arrow to what it connects to, in YOUR label, not the textbook's. (Still a full sentence = not encoded yet.)
5. **Feynman close** — say it out loud, notes CLOSED. Fix only where you stumble.

## TRACK B — Reactivate a RUSTY concept (your main case, ~5–8 min)
1. **Blank-page recall FIRST** — from memory: the limit, the neighbor-contrast, the analogy. The act of pulling it through working memory re-strengthens it more than re-reading ever could.
2. **Patch only the miss** — 60 sec notes, fix the one thing you blanked, close, re-state.
3. **Curveball** — answer a NEW scenario that combines it with a second concept. If you can only answer the original framing, it's brittle.
→ **Your rep IS the reactivation. No separate study needed.**

## TRACK C — Spacing (so it compounds)
- Recall at **Day 1 → Week 1 → Month 1**, then let it go organic: **using it in a real design/debug = the review** (name which concept you're applying).
- Drive each morning's rep off a **due-date list**; pull whichever concept is due (new or rusty) into that day's scenario.
- **Interleave the two domains** — mix SD and AE reps in the same week (A-B-A-B), not a week of each. Feels slower, sticks harder. Fold a rusty concept into each new scenario.

## The one rule
**NEVER re-read. Replace every re-read with a blank-page recall.** Re-reading is the illusion of learning.
