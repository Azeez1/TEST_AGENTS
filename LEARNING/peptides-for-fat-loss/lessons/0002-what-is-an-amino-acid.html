What is an amino acid?

The small molecule you need to understand before a peptide makes sense.

Start with atoms

An atom is a tiny unit of an element, such as carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, or hydrogen. Atoms can attach to one another. An attached group of atoms is a molecule.

An amino acid is a particular kind of molecule. The amino acids your body uses to build proteins share a common layout: a central carbon attached to an amino group, a carboxyl group, a hydrogen, and a variable side group. Source: OpenStax Biology

The drawing is a simplified formula; in the watery conditions inside your body, the charged forms of these groups are common.

Why the name?

Amino points to the nitrogen-containing amino group. Acid points to the carboxyl group, which can give up a hydrogen ion. The R side group changes from one amino acid to another. About 20 common amino acids are used to build human proteins. Source: NHGRI · Source: OpenStax

Same basic frame; different side group. That difference helps each amino acid behave differently when combined with others.
